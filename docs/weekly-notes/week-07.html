House price prediction

From tracts to houses

same ML methods, different unit of analysis.

before we used census tracts (polygons), now individual house sales (points).

~400 tracts vs ~20,000 houses.

crime is counted within a 660 ft buffer around each house instead of within a tract. can also use mean distance to the 5 nearest crimes (kNN).

Cleaning the sales

started with 37,866 recorded sales from 2023–2024.

not every recorded sale is an actual market sale. some are $1 family transfers, sheriff sales, LLC transfers, etc.

sales <- sales %>%
  filter(sale_price >= 10000, total_livable_area > 0, !st_is_empty(.))

also remove missing building age and bathrooms.

final = 27,551 sales.

deciding what counts as a market sale is a modeling decision.

Feature engineering

two ways to measure violent crime near a house.

violent_660ft = number of violent crimes within 660 ft.

violent_knn5 = mean distance to 5 nearest violent crimes.

buffer counts can be zero for many houses, while kNN distance generally isn’t.

which is better? let cross-validation RMSE decide.

Where is the model wrong?

Residuals

m1 <- lm(sale_price ~ total_livable_area + number_of_bathrooms +
           age + violent_knn5, data = sales)

sales <- sales %>%
  mutate(pred = predict(m1, sales),
         resid = sale_price - pred)

residual = actual − predicted

positive residual = actual higher than predicted, model guessed too low.

negative residual = actual lower than predicted, model guessed too high.

map residuals to see where the model is making mistakes.

alpha helps with overlapping points, squish stops extreme residuals from dominating the colors.

Spatial autocorrelation

Tobler’s law = nearby things tend to be more related than distant things.

if nearby houses have similar residuals, the model may be missing something about location.

Spatial lag

spatial lag = average residual of neighboring houses.

coords <- st_coordinates(sales)

w_knn <- nb2listw(knn2nb(knearneigh(coords, k = 5)), style = "W")

sales$resid_lag <- lag.listw(w_knn, sales$resid)

k = 5 finds the 5 nearest neighbors.

style = “W” makes the weights sum to 1 for each house.

resid_lag = average residual of those neighbors.

main idea

if nearby houses have related prediction errors, the errors are spatially clustered rather than independent. the model may be missing geographic factors.

Me vs. my neighbors

ggplot(sales, aes(resid, resid_lag)) +
  geom_point(alpha = 0.15, size = 0.6) +
  geom_smooth(method = "lm", se = FALSE, color = "#b2182b") +
  coord_cartesian(xlim = c(-3e5, 3e5), ylim = c(-2e5, 2e5)) +
  labs(x = "My residual", y = "Average of my 5 neighbors' residuals")

x = my residual, y = average residual of my 5 nearest neighbors.

the slope of the line is Moran’s I when using row-standardized weights and an intercept.

Moran’s I

measures spatial autocorrelation, or whether nearby observations have similar values.

\[ I = \frac{ \sum_i \sum_j w_{ij}(x_i - \bar{x})(x_j - \bar{x}) }{ \sum_i (x_i - \bar{x})^2 } \]

basically asking: if my residual is above average, are my neighbors’ residuals above average too?

positive Moran’s I = nearby houses tend to have similar residuals.

negative Moran’s I = nearby houses tend to have opposite residuals.

near zero = little spatial autocorrelation.

Five houses example

house residual deviation from mean
A +100k +84k
B +80k +64k
C +20k +4k
D −50k −66k
E −70k −86k

mean residual = +16k.

deviation = residual − mean.

positive residual = model underpredicted the house price.

houses A and B both have large positive residuals, while D and E both have negative residuals.

if these houses are next to each other in that order, similar errors are clustering together.

Moran’s I - five houses example

five houses on a block, arranged A → B → C → D → E.

house residual ($k) deviation from mean
A +100 +84
B +80 +64
C +20 +4
D −50 −66
E −70 −86

mean residual = +16k.

we subtract the mean from each residual because Moran’s I compares whether neighboring houses are above or below the average.

Formula

\[ I = \frac{ \sum_i \sum_j w_{ij}(x_i-\bar{x})(x_j-\bar{x}) }{ \sum_i (x_i-\bar{x})^2 } \]

Step 1 - calculate the denominator

square each house’s deviation from the mean and add them together.

\[ 84^2 + 64^2 + 4^2 + (-66)^2 + (-86)^2 \]

\[ = 7056 + 4096 + 16 + 4356 + 7396 = 22920 \]

Step 2 - calculate the numerator

each house is compared to its immediate neighbors.

A and E have 1 neighbor, so their weight is 1.

B, C, and D have 2 neighbors, so each neighbor gets a weight of 0.5.

\[ \begin{aligned} A &: 1(84)(64) = 5376 \\ B &: 0.5(64)(84) + 0.5(64)(4) = 2816 \\ C &: 0.5(4)(64) + 0.5(4)(-66) = -4 \\ D &: 0.5(-66)(4) + 0.5(-66)(-86) = 2706 \\ E &: 1(-86)(-66) = 5676 \end{aligned} \]

add all the neighbor products.

\[ 5376 + 2816 - 4 + 2706 + 5676 = 16570 \]

Step 3 - calculate Moran’s I

\[ I = \frac{16570}{22920} \approx 0.723 \]

Interpretation

Moran’s I ≈ +0.72

this indicates positive spatial autocorrelation.

houses A and B both have large positive residuals, while D and E both have negative residuals.

nearby houses are making similar prediction errors, so the model may be missing something about location.

main idea

positive Moran’s I = similar residuals cluster together.

negative Moran’s I = neighboring residuals tend to be different.

near zero = little spatial autocorrelation.

main idea

Moran’s I tells us whether prediction errors are spatially related instead of randomly distributed.

if residuals cluster geographically, the model may be missing something about location.

## Moran's I

measures spatial autocorrelation, or whether nearby observations have similar values.

$$
I = \frac{
\sum_i \sum_j w_{ij}(x_i - \bar{x})(x_j - \bar{x})
}{
\sum_i (x_i - \bar{x})^2
}
$$

basically asking: **if my residual is above average, are my neighbors' residuals above average too?**

positive Moran's I = nearby houses tend to have similar residuals.

negative Moran's I = nearby houses tend to have opposite residuals.

near zero = little spatial autocorrelation.

## Moran's I - five houses example

five houses on a block, arranged A → B → C → D → E.

| house | residual (\$k) | deviation from mean |
|-------|----------------|---------------------|
| A     | +100           | +84                 |
| B     | +80            | +64                 |
| C     | +20            | +4                  |
| D     | −50            | −66                 |
| E     | −70            | −86                 |

mean residual = +16k.

we subtract the mean from each residual because Moran's I compares whether neighboring houses are above or below the average.

### Formula

$$
I = \frac{
\sum_i \sum_j w_{ij}(x_i-\bar{x})(x_j-\bar{x})
}{
\sum_i (x_i-\bar{x})^2
}
$$

### Step 1 - calculate the denominator

square each house's deviation from the mean and add them together.

$$
84^2 + 64^2 + 4^2 + (-66)^2 + (-86)^2
$$

$$
= 7056 + 4096 + 16 + 4356 + 7396
= 22920
$$

### Step 2 - calculate the numerator

each house is compared to its immediate neighbors.

A and E have 1 neighbor, so their weight is 1.

B, C, and D have 2 neighbors, so each neighbor gets a weight of 0.5.

$$
\begin{aligned}
A &: 1(84)(64) = 5376 \\
B &: 0.5(64)(84) + 0.5(64)(4) = 2816 \\
C &: 0.5(4)(64) + 0.5(4)(-66) = -4 \\
D &: 0.5(-66)(4) + 0.5(-66)(-86) = 2706 \\
E &: 1(-86)(-66) = 5676
\end{aligned}
$$

add all the neighbor products.

$$
5376 + 2816 - 4 + 2706 + 5676 = 16570
$$

### Step 3 - calculate Moran's I

$$
I = \frac{16570}{22920}
\approx 0.723
$$

### Interpretation

**Moran's I ≈ +0.72**

this indicates positive spatial autocorrelation.

houses A and B both have large positive residuals, while D and E both have negative residuals.

nearby houses are making similar prediction errors, so the model may be missing something about location.

**main idea**

positive Moran's I = similar residuals cluster together.

negative Moran's I = neighboring residuals tend to be different.

near zero = little spatial autocorrelation.

**main idea**

Moran's I tells us whether prediction errors are spatially related instead of randomly distributed.

if residuals cluster geographically, the model may be missing something about location.
