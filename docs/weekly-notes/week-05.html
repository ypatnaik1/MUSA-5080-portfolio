Y = f(x) + ε

y = rent

f(x) = function unknown

ε = everything else

an example of some sort of factor is we all live in University City / West Philly and have low income for the expected rent from the function due to our status as students

Rigid: easier to explain and interpret

Flexible

rigid: linear, lasso/ridge

neutral: logistic

flexible: trees, random forest, boosting, neural networks

Parametric

Assume a functional form

Non-Parametric

Don’t (?)

\(\hat{y}= b_0+b_1x_1 + ε\)

\(\hat{y}\) = the prediction

\(b_0+b_1x_1 + ε\) = the inference

Least Squares

Minimize Sum of Squared Errors

The code -

m1 <- lm(med_rent ~ med_inc, data=tracts)

summary(m1)

Estimate S.E. t-value p-value
(intercept) 690 38.0 18.2 <2e-16
med_inc 0.0081 0.0005 16.2 <2e-16

0.0081 = Dollars of monthly rent per dollar of annual income

0.0081*1,000 = +$81/month in predicted rent ***

Noise

The noise in Y goes in the residuals; systematic variation (not noise)

The noise in X flattens the slope

Fit vs. Prediction

\(R^2\) = On the data it was fit to

Test/Train= 80% of your data for the training phase, come up with your best line, leave out 20% for the test

19000 -> Estimate 500 -400 = off by 100

29000 -> Estimate 900 -1200

= off by 300

RMSE (root mean square error) -> lower is better
